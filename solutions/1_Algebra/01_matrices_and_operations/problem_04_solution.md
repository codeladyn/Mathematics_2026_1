### Exercise 4. Matrix Multiplication and Noncommutativity

**Result**

$$
\begin{aligned}
AB &=
\begin{pmatrix}
1(2)+2(3) & 1(0)+2(1) \\
0(2)+1(3) & 0(0)+1(1) \\
\end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
8 & 2 \\
3 & 1 \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
BA &=
\begin{pmatrix}
2(1)+0(0) & 2(2)+0(1) \\
3(1)+1(0) & 3(2)+1(1) \\
\end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
2 & 4 \\
3 & 7 \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Explanation**

In normal math, $2 \times 3$ is the same as $3 \times 2$. In matrix math, order matters! As you can see here, $AB$ is completely different from $BA$. This is what "noncommutativity" means.
