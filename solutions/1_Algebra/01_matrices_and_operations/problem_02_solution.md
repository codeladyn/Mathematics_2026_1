### Exercise 2. Addition and Scalar Multiplication

## Question

For the matrices:

$$
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 2 \\
-1 & 3 \\
\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=
\begin{pmatrix}
4 & -2 \\
0 & 5 \\
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Compute $A+B$, $A-B$, and $3A-2B$.
Explain why matrix addition is possible only for matrices of the same size.

## Solution

$$
\begin{aligned}
A+B &=
\begin{pmatrix}
1+4 & 2-2 \\
-1+0 & 3+5 \\
\end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
5 & 0 \\
-1 & 8 \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
A-B &=
\begin{pmatrix}
1-4 & 2-(-2) \\
-1-0 & 3-5 \\
\end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
-3 & 4 \\
-1 & -2 \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
3A-2B &=
\begin{pmatrix}
3 & 6 \\
-3 & 9 \\
\end{pmatrix}
-
\begin{pmatrix}
8 & -4 \\
0 & 10 \\
\end{pmatrix} \\
&=
\begin{pmatrix}
-5 & 10 \\
-3 & -1 \\
\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

To add or subtract matrices, you just match up the positions and do the math number by number. Because of this, they have to be the exact same shape and size. When you multiply a matrix by a regular number (a scalar), you just multiply every single piece inside by that number.
