### Exercise 9. Distances

## Question

Compute:
1. the distance from $P=(2,3)$ to the line $3x+4y-10=0$,
2. the distance from $Q=(2,-1,3)$ to the plane $2x-2y+z-4=0$.

Compare the two formulas and explain their common connection with the normal direction.

## Solution

1. Distance from $P=(2,3)$ to $3x + 4y - 10 = 0$:

$$
d_1 = \frac{|3(2) + 4(3) - 10|}{\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}} = \frac{|6 + 12 - 10|}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{8}{5}
$$

2. Distance from $Q=(2,-1,3)$ to $2x - 2y + z - 4 = 0$:

$$
d_2 = \frac{|2(2) - 2(-1) + 1(3) - 4|}{\sqrt{2^2 + (-2)^2 + 1^2}} = \frac{|4 + 2 + 3 - 4|}{\sqrt{9}} = \frac{5}{3}
$$

Both formulas have the exact same structure: plug the point into the linear equation, take the absolute value, and divide by the length of the normal vector $\|n\|$. They are the same because the shortest distance to a line or a plane is always measured straight along its normal direction (projecting onto the unit normal vector).
