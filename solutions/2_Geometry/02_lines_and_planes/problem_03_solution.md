### Exercise 3. Plane Through a Point with a Given Normal Vector

## Question

Find the equation of the plane through $P=(1,-2,3)$ and perpendicular to $n=(2,1,-1)$. Check the result by substituting the point $P$ and explain the role of the normal vector.

## Solution

Using the point-normal equation of a plane, $n\cdot((x,y,z) - P) = 0$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
2(x - 1) + 1(y - (-2)) - 1(z - 3) &= 0 \\
2x - 2 + y + 2 - z + 3 &= 0 \\
2x + y - z + 3 &= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

The equation of the plane is $2x + y - z + 3 = 0$ (or $2x + y - z = -3$).

Check by substituting $P=(1,-2,3)$:

$$
2(1) + (-2) - 3 + 3 = 2 - 2 - 3 + 3 = 0
$$

The point satisfies the equation. The normal vector $n$ fixes the tilt (orientation) of the plane in space: every vector lying inside the plane must be perpendicular to $n$.
