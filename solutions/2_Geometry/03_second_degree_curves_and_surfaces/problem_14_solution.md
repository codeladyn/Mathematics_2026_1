### Exercise 14. Cone and Cylinder

## Question

Compare

$$
x^2+y^2=z^2
$$

and

$$
x^2+y^2=4.
$$

For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

## Solution

**Surface 1: $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ (Circular cone)**
- $z = c$: $x^2 + y^2 = c^2$, a circle of radius $|c|$ (or the single point $(0,0,0)$ when $c = 0$).
- $x = 0$: $y^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm y$ (two straight lines crossing at the origin).
- $y = 0$: $x^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm x$ (two straight lines crossing at the origin).
- **Sketch:** A double cone meeting at its tip at $(0,0,0)$, widening linearly upward and downward.

**Surface 2: $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ (Circular cylinder)**
- $z = c$: $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, a circle of constant radius $2$ at every height.
- $x = 0$: $y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2$ (two vertical parallel lines).
- $y = 0$: $x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2$ (two vertical parallel lines).
- **Sketch:** A vertical tube of radius $2$ centered on the $z$-axis extending infinitely up and down.

In $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$, the variable $z$ appears explicitly on the right-hand side, forcing the radius $|z|$ to grow as you move away from $z = 0$. In $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, the variable $z$ does not appear at all, so the circular cross-section has the exact same radius $2$ regardless of height.
