### Exercise 3. Ellipse

## Question

For

$$
\frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
$$

find the semiaxis lengths, the intercepts with the coordinate axes, and make a sketch. Explain along which axis the ellipse is more stretched and how this is visible in the equation.

## Solution

From the canonical form $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$:
- Semiaxis along the $x$-axis: $a = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
- Semiaxis along the $y$-axis: $b = \sqrt{9} = 3$.

Intercepts with the coordinate axes:
- Set $y = 0$: $\frac{x^2}{25} = 1 \implies x = \pm 5$, giving $(-5, 0)$ and $(5, 0)$.
- Set $x = 0$: $\frac{y^2}{9} = 1 \implies y = \pm 3$, giving $(0, -3)$ and $(0, 3)$.

The ellipse is more stretched along the **$x$-axis** because the larger denominator ($25 > 9$) is under $x^2$, which makes the horizontal semiaxis ($5$) longer than the vertical semiaxis ($3$).

**Sketch:** A horizontal oval centered at $(0,0)$ spanning from $x=-5$ to $x=5$ and from $y=-3$ to $y=3$.
