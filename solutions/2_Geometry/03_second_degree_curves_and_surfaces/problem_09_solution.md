### Exercise 9. Ellipsoid and Its Cross-Sections

## Question

Consider:

$$
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}+\frac{z^2}{16}=1.
$$

Give the semiaxis lengths, describe the cross-section $z=0$, and explain how the cross-sections $z=c$ change for $|c|<4$.

## Solution

- **Semiaxis lengths:** $a = \sqrt{9} = 3$ (along $x$), $b = \sqrt{4} = 2$ (along $y$), and $c_0 = \sqrt{16} = 4$ (along $z$).
- **Cross-section $z = 0$:** Setting $z = 0$ gives:

$$
\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1
$$

This is an ellipse in the $xy$-plane with semiaxes $3$ and $2$.

- **Cross-sections $z = c$ for $|c| < 4$:** Substituting $z = c$ gives:

$$
\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1 - \frac{c^2}{16}
$$

Because $|c| < 4$, the right-hand side is positive. Every horizontal slice is an ellipse with the same shape (aspect ratio $3:2$) as the $z = 0$ slice, but as $|c|$ increases from $0$ toward $4$, the right-hand side gets smaller, so the ellipses shrink smoothly until they collapse to single points at $z = \pm 4$.
