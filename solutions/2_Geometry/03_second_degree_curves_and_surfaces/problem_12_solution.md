### Exercise 12. Recognizing a Surface from Cross-Sections

## Question

A certain surface has the following properties:
- the cross-section $z=0$ is a point,
- for $z>0$, the cross-sections $z=\mathrm{const}$ are circles with increasing radius,
- vertical cross-sections through the $Oz$ axis are parabolas.

Propose a simple equation for such a surface and justify your choice.

## Solution

A simple equation for this surface is:

$$
z = x^2 + y^2
$$

**Justification:**
- At $z = 0$: $x^2 + y^2 = 0$, which has only the single solution $(0, 0, 0)$ (a point).
- For $z = c > 0$: $x^2 + y^2 = c$ is a circle of radius $\sqrt{c}$, which grows larger as $c$ increases.
- Vertical cross-sections through the $Oz$ axis (such as $x = 0$ giving $z = y^2$, or $y = 0$ giving $z = x^2$) are upward-opening parabolas.
