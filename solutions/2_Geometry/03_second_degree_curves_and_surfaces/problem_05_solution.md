### Exercise 5. Hyperbola and Asymptotes

## Question

For

$$
\frac{x^2}{16}-\frac{y^2}{9}=1,
$$

find the intercepts with the $x$-axis, the asymptotes, and make a sketch. Explain why the asymptotes describe the behavior of the curve far from the center.

## Solution

Intercepts with the $x$-axis (set $y = 0$):

$$
\frac{x^2}{16} = 1 \implies x = \pm 4
$$

The $x$-intercepts (vertices) are $(-4, 0)$ and $(4, 0)$.

Asymptotes (replace $1$ with $0$):

$$
\frac{x^2}{16} - \frac{y^2}{9} = 0 \implies y^2 = \frac{9}{16}x^2 \implies y = \pm \frac{3}{4}x
$$

Far from the center (when $|x|$ and $|y|$ are very large), the constant $1$ on the right-hand side becomes tiny compared to $\frac{x^2}{16}$ and $\frac{y^2}{9}$. Thus $\frac{x^2}{16} - \frac{y^2}{9} \approx 0$, which means the hyperbola gets arbitrarily close to the straight lines $y = \pm \frac{3}{4}x$.

**Sketch:** Draw the dashed lines $y = \frac{3}{4}x$ and $y = -\frac{3}{4}x$ crossing at $(0,0)$. Draw two branches starting at $(-4,0)$ and $(4,0)$ opening outward to the left and right, hugging the dashed asymptotes as they extend away from the origin.
