### Exercise 2. Circle by Completing the Square

## Question

Reduce

$$
x^2+y^2-6x+4y-3=0
$$

to canonical form. Find the center and radius, then substitute two simple points from the circle into both forms of the equation.

## Solution

Group the $x$-terms and $y$-terms and complete the square:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(x^2 - 6x) + (y^2 + 4y) &= 3 \\
(x - 3)^2 - 9 + (y + 2)^2 - 4 &= 3 \\
(x - 3)^2 + (y + 2)^2 &= 16
\end{aligned}
$$

This is a circle with center $(3, -2)$ and radius $r = \sqrt{16} = 4$.

Two simple points on the circle are found by moving $4$ units right and $4$ units up from the center: $P_1 = (7, -2)$ and $P_2 = (3, 2)$.

Check $P_1 = (7, -2)$:
- Canonical form: $(7 - 3)^2 + (-2 + 2)^2 = 4^2 + 0 = 16$ (true).
- Original form: $7^2 + (-2)^2 - 6(7) + 4(-2) - 3 = 49 + 4 - 42 - 8 - 3 = 0$ (true).

Check $P_2 = (3, 2)$:
- Canonical form: $(3 - 3)^2 + (2 + 2)^2 = 0 + 4^2 = 16$ (true).
- Original form: $3^2 + 2^2 - 6(3) + 4(2) - 3 = 9 + 4 - 18 + 8 - 3 = 0$ (true).
