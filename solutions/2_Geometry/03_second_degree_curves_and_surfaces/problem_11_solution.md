### Exercise 11. Two Similar Surfaces

## Question

Compare

$$
x^2+y^2-z^2=1
$$

and

$$
x^2+y^2-z^2=-1.
$$

For several values $z=c$, check whether the cross-section exists and what its radius is. Based on this, describe qualitatively the difference between the surfaces and make sketches.

## Solution

**Surface 1: $x^2 + y^2 - z^2 = 1 \implies x^2 + y^2 = 1 + c^2$**
- $z = 0$: $x^2 + y^2 = 1$ (circle of radius $1$).
- $z = \pm 1$: $x^2 + y^2 = 2$ (circle of radius $\sqrt{2}$).
- $z = \pm 2$: $x^2 + y^2 = 5$ (circle of radius $\sqrt{5}$).

Because $1 + c^2 \ge 1$ for every $c$, a circular cross-section exists at every height. The surface is a single connected hourglass shape (a **one-sheeted hyperboloid**) with its narrowest waist of radius $1$ at $z = 0$.

**Surface 2: $x^2 + y^2 - z^2 = -1 \implies x^2 + y^2 = c^2 - 1$**
- $z = 0$: $x^2 + y^2 = -1$ (does not exist — empty).
- $z = \pm 1$: $x^2 + y^2 = 0$ (single points $(0,0,1)$ and $(0,0,-1)$, radius $0$).
- $z = \pm 2$: $x^2 + y^2 = 3$ (circle of radius $\sqrt{3}$).

Cross-sections exist only when $|c| \ge 1$. There is a complete gap between $z = -1$ and $z = 1$, splitting the surface into two separate bowl-like pieces opening upward and downward (a **two-sheeted hyperboloid**).
